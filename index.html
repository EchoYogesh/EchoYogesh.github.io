Sunday, 31 January

How the day unfolds

The baraat arrives at the muhurat of four o'clock. After that the rituals set the pace, not the clock.

  1. Baraat

    Four o'clock, at the gate: dhol, dancing and rather more people than planned.

  2. Milni

    Both families meet at the entrance with garlands and embraces. This is where the noise begins.

  3. Jaimala

    Brijesh and Sangeeta exchange garlands, and the pandit begins the ceremony.

  4. Kanyadaan & pheras

    The seven vows around the fire. Please gather close — this is the part worth watching.

  5. Sindoor & mangalsutra

    Blessings, a few tears, and the moment the two of them are formally married.

  6. Dinner

    A long vegetarian spread — chaat, sabzi, dal, hot puris and far too much dessert.

  7. Vidaai

    Sangeeta leaves with her new family. Bring a handkerchief.

The ceremony will be conducted in Hindi and Sanskrit. We'll keep it easy to follow, and there will be someone at every row to explain what is happening.

Travel & stay

Reaching Jhaliyahwa

The village sits in Pratapgarh district, between Lucknow and Prayagraj. Trains are the easiest way in; from the station, we'll help with the last hour.

By train

Pratapgarh Junction is the closest station, with direct trains from Lucknow, Prayagraj, Delhi and Varanasi. Tell us your train and we'll arrange a car from the station.

By air

Prayagraj and Lucknow are the nearest airports, both a few hours by road. If several guests arrive at once, we'll organise a shared car — let us know your flight.

By road

The village is off the Chandauka road in Kohandaur. From Lucknow or Prayagraj, take the highway towards Pratapgarh and turn at Chandauka; ask for the Yadav house and anyone will point you on.

Where to stay

We are keeping rooms at home for close family, and there are guest houses in Pratapgarh town twenty minutes away. Tell us in the RSVP and we'll find you somewhere comfortable.

Questions

Things people have asked

What should I wear?

Traditional Indian festive, and the brighter the better — sarees, lehengas, kurtas, sherwanis, or a smart suit. Please avoid all-black at the rituals. The pheras involve sitting on the floor, so choose something you can fold into.

Anything particular for the haldi?

Yellow or marigold if you have it, and clothes you don't mind staining — turmeric does not wash out. Sandals rather than anything precious.

And for the mehndi?

Come in the evening, wear something green if it suits you, and eat first — the mehndi takes a while to dry, so the plates come out early.

How far is everything from each other?

All four functions are at the family home in Jhaliyahwa. The haldi is in the courtyard, the mehndi on the veranda, the wedding under the mandap beside it, and the reception under the shamiana — a two-minute walk in each case.

Where do I stay?

Close family stay at the house. There are guest houses in Pratapgarh town, twenty minutes away, and we will arrange a room and a car for anyone who needs one — just say so in the RSVP.

Is there food for everyone?

The kitchen is pure vegetarian, with Jain and vegan plates on request. If you have an allergy, put it in the RSVP and it will be kept aside for you.

Can I bring my children?

Of course; weddings here are family affairs. Tell us how many of you are coming so the places and the small plates can be counted.

What is the weather like in January?

Cold mornings and fog, mild afternoons, and properly cold evenings — bring a shawl or a jacket, and shoes that can manage a village lane.
